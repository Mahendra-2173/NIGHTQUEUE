# Research: NIT Silchar Late-Night Stall Queue Delay & Hostel Curfew Risk

**Person:** Bipul Nath ("Bipul Da")  
**Role:** Sole proprietor and cook at *Bipul Da's Night Canteen & Tea Stall*, situated exactly 80 meters outside Gate No. 1 (Main Gate), National Institute of Technology (NIT) Silchar, Cachar, Assam (788010).  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  

---

### 1. The Problem (in 5 lines)
Hostel students at NIT Silchar (Hostel 6, Hostel 7, and Hostel 8) face a strict late-night hostel register sign-in and gate curfew at 00:30 AM (12:30 AM) enforced by campus security guards. 
During late study hours, dozens of hostellers walk 400–600 meters to Bipul Da's stall outside Gate 1 for dinner snacks (Egg Maggi, Bun Omelette, Lal Saah/Chai). 
Bipul Da cooks on a simple 2-burner commercial gas stove and systematically underestimates preparation delays, telling every anxious student "bas 5 minute lagega, bohi thaak (sit down)". 
When 6 to 8 orders queue up, actual cooking and pan-clearing takes 20 to 28 minutes, causing students to run across the dark campus road in panic to beat the hostel guard's whistle. 
Bipul Da faces constant counter shouting, students canceling orders midway when the 00:15 AM warning bell strikes, and chaotic disputes over UPI payments.

---

### 2. Existing Solutions & Why They Do Not Fit Bipul Da

1. **Food Aggregators (Zomato / Swiggy)**
   * *Why it does not fit:* Delivery services in the Silchar outskirts (near NIT campus) do not accept late-night micro-orders (₹35–₹60) from unregistered roadside stalls, and the high delivery charges and minimum order values are rejected by hostellers.

2. **WhatsApp / Phone Calling**
   * *Why it does not fit:* Bipul Da cannot hold a smartphone or read texts while stirring boiling Maggi pans with one hand and cracking eggs with the other amid ambient traffic noise on the Silchar-Hailakandi Road.

### 3. Two Sources Read (with 3-line notes each)

1. **Little, J. D. C. (1961). *A Proof for the Queuing Formula: $L = \lambda W$*. Operations Research, 9(3), 383–387.**
   * *Note 1:* Formulates the fundamental queuing theorem that long-term average queue length ($L$) equals steady-state arrival rate ($\lambda$) multiplied by average waiting time ($W$).
   * *Note 2:* Establishes that wait time escalates non-linearly when customer arrival rate approaches service capacity across Bipul Da's 2 commercial burners.
   * *Note 3:* Demonstrates that Bipul Da's universal "5-minute" promise is physically and mathematically impossible once pending queue depth exceeds 2 tickets.

2. **Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979). *Intuitive prediction: Biases and fallacious interpretations*. In M. K. Starr & M. Zeleny (Eds.), Multiple Criteria Decision Making, TIMS Studies in the Management Sciences (Vol. 12, pp. 313–327). Amsterdam: North-Holland Publishing Company.**
   * *Note 1:* Formally defines the *planning fallacy*—the cognitive heuristic where human estimators anchor on an idealized best-case scenario while systematically ignoring distribution of historical completion times and operational friction.
   * *Note 2:* Explains why roadside vendors instinctively quote "5 minutes" to prevent customers from walking away to competing stalls, despite knowing past orders routinely took 18–25 minutes.
   * *Note 3:* Provides the theoretical justification for replacing human verbal promises with an empirical, data-calibrated algorithmic estimator that protects students from curfew lockouts.
