      The Story Behind the Stall

      Fieldwork conducted at NIT Silchar Main Gate, Cachar, Assam.

      📍

      The Place

      Roadside tin-shed stall, exactly 80 meters outside Gate 1 (Main Gate) of NIT Silchar, Cachar, Assam. Hostellers from Hostel 1 through 9 walk across campus in the freezing night during exam weeks for late-night food.

      ⏰

      The 12:30 AM Curfew

      Campus security strictly enforces a 12:30 AM gate register sign-in. At 12:20 AM, guards sound warning whistles. Anyone arriving after 12:30 AM has their name and roll number recorded in the penalty register.

      🗣️

      The "5-Minute" Habit

      Bipul Da (age 44) always tells students: "Bas 5 minute lagega, bohi thaak!" He explains: if he admits food will take 20 minutes, impatient students will walk away to other tea stalls or skip dinner.

      🍳 Fieldwork Findings & The Pan Transition Latency

      During our fieldwork on November 14–15, we observed that cooking speed alone wasn't the only bottleneck. What really multiplied delays was pan changeover overhead:

      • Bipul Da uses only two commercial burners: one for boiling Maggi and one tawa for frying eggs and buns.
      • Whenever an order switches from Maggi to Egg Toast, he has to wipe egg grease off the tawa with a cloth, re-apply oil, and whisk fresh eggs. This dead time takes 60 to 90 seconds between discordant food categories.
      • When 6 to 8 orders accumulate, cumulative wait times surge past 20 minutes, causing panic among students heading back to distant hostels (Hostels 7, 8, and PG Hostel 9).
      Assignment Evidence: Two timestamped photographs (photos/nit_silchar_stall_setup.jpg at 23:28 IST and photos/rush_hour_burners.jpg at 23:55 IST) accompany the fieldwork report in fieldwork.md.
